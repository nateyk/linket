DNS sounds intimidating, but the records involved in connecting a domain are few and simple. Here is what each one does.

CNAME record

A CNAME maps one domain name to another. Yours points your subdomain (like go.yourbrand.com) at our servers, so every short link lives on your brand's domain while we handle the redirects.

A record

An A record points a hostname directly at an IPv4 address. You only need one if your registrar asks for it instead of a CNAME — we show the correct value in that case.

TXT record

A TXT record holds arbitrary text — we use it to confirm you control the domain. It has no effect on visitors; it exists purely for verification.

Nameservers vs records

Nameservers decide where your DNS is managed; records decide what each hostname resolves to. If you have ever switched registrars, you may have moved nameservers — make sure you add these records wherever your nameservers currently point.

Propagation

After any change, resolvers around the world need time to pick it up — usually minutes, occasionally up to 24 hours. This is normal and not a sign that something is wrong.
